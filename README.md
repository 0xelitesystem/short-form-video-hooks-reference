# Short-Form Video Hooks

A plain-language reference on hooks for short-form video on Shorts, Reels, and TikTok: the first one to three seconds that decide whether a viewer stays or scrolls. It covers why the opening matters so much, the main hook types, how hooks connect to retention, and concrete patterns you can adapt.

## The core idea

A hook is the opening moment of a short video, roughly the first one to three seconds, whose only job is to stop the scroll and make the viewer want the next second. Short-form platforms feed videos into a fast-scrolling feed where viewers decide almost instantly whether to keep watching, so a weak opening loses people before the content begins. Strong hooks work by creating an immediate reason to stay: a question, a bold claim, a result shown first, or a visual that breaks the pattern. Retention starts at second one, and the hook is what protects it.

## The stance

This reference treats the hook as a promise, not a trick. The opening must earn attention and then the rest of the video must pay off what the opening implied. A hook that overpromises and underdelivers trains viewers to distrust you, so the goal is a true, compelling opening, not bait.
