# Retention after the hook

A hook only buys the first few seconds; retention is keeping the viewer through the rest of the video. The opening stops the scroll, but the body has to deliver on what the hook promised and keep giving the viewer reasons to stay to the end. On short-form platforms, watching to completion and rewatching are strong signals that push the video to more people.

## The hook makes a promise the body must keep

Every hook implies a payoff: the question implies an answer, the result-first implies the method, the contrarian implies the argument. If the body wanders, stalls, or never delivers that payoff, the viewer leaves and learns not to trust your openings. Retention depends on the body honoring the promise the hook made, ideally building toward it so the viewer stays to reach it. The hook and the body are one structure, not two separate jobs.

## Pace and remove dead air

Short-form viewers have almost no patience for slack. Cut pauses, filler words, and slow transitions so the video moves continuously and never gives the viewer a flat moment to scroll past. Tight editing, quick cuts where they serve the content, and a steady forward motion keep attention. The question to ask of every second is whether it earns its place; if a moment can be cut without losing meaning, cutting it usually helps retention.

## Build curiosity loops through the middle

Beyond the opening hook, you can plant small open loops that pull the viewer forward: previewing something coming later, counting down to a final point, or teasing a result still ahead. Each loop gives a reason to stay a little longer, and closing them in sequence carries the viewer to the end. The technique is the same as the opening hook, applied in miniature throughout, so the video never runs out of reasons to keep watching.

## Land the ending and invite a rewatch

The final seconds matter because completion is a strong signal and because a clean ending can prompt a rewatch or a loop. Deliver the payoff clearly, avoid trailing off, and on looping platforms consider an ending that flows back into the opening so the video plays again seamlessly. A satisfying close, or a loop that rewards a second viewing, turns a single watch into the kind of engagement the feed rewards with wider reach.
