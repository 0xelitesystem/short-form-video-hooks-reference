# The first three seconds

The first one to three seconds of a short-form video decide whether the viewer keeps watching or scrolls away. On Shorts, Reels, and TikTok, videos play automatically in a fast feed, and viewers judge almost instantly whether yours is worth their time. Everything good in the video is wasted if the opening does not earn that first decision.

## Why the opening carries so much weight

Short-form feeds are built for rapid scrolling, so a viewer is one thumb-flick away from the next video at all times. That makes the cost of a slow start brutal: a few seconds of logo, throat-clearing, or setup, and a large share of viewers are gone before the substance arrives. The opening is not a warm-up; it is the gate. If it does not stop the scroll, nothing after it gets seen.

## The platforms reward early retention

These platforms decide how widely to show a video partly by how well it holds viewers, and the steepest drop-off is usually right at the start. A video that loses half its audience in the first two seconds signals a weak fit, and the feed shows it to fewer people. A video that holds viewers through the opening earns more distribution. Because the algorithm watches early retention closely, the hook is not just a creative choice; it directly shapes how far the video travels.

## Lead with the payoff, not the setup

The instinct to introduce yourself, set context, and build up is borrowed from longer formats and works against you here. In short form, start at the most interesting point: the result, the claim, the question, the surprising image. Context can come a beat later, once the viewer has decided to stay. Cutting the runway so the first frame is already compelling is the single highest-leverage edit most short videos need.

## Make the first frame and first words count

Both the visual and the audio of the opening matter, because some viewers watch muted and some listen. The first frame should be visually arresting or clearly promise something, and the first spoken words should state the hook plainly. On-screen text in the opening helps muted viewers grasp the promise instantly. Treat the first frame, the first line, and the first caption as one coordinated hook, all pointing at the reason to keep watching.
