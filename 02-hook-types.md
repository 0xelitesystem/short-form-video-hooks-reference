# Hook types

Most effective short-form hooks fall into a few recognizable types: the question, the contrarian take, the result-first reveal, and the pattern interrupt. Each creates a different reason to keep watching, an open loop, a challenge to belief, a payoff shown up front, or a jolt of surprise. Knowing the types lets you choose the right opening for the content instead of guessing.

## The question hook

A question hook opens by asking something the viewer wants answered, creating an open loop their mind wants to close. "Why does your coffee taste bitter?" or "What is the one mistake new runners make?" sets up a gap between what the viewer knows and what they want to know. The promise is the answer, and the viewer stays to get it. The question must be one the audience actually cares about and one the video genuinely answers, or the loop feels cheap.

## The contrarian hook

A contrarian hook opens by challenging a common belief, which stops viewers who hold that belief and makes them want to defend or reconsider it. "Stretching before a run is a waste of time" or "You are saving money the wrong way" provokes a reaction precisely because it pushes against the assumed answer. It works because disagreement is engaging, but it carries a duty: you must back the claim in the video. A contrarian hook with no real argument behind it reads as empty provocation.

## The result-first hook

A result-first hook shows the outcome before the process, leading with the finished thing so the viewer wants to see how it was done. The transformed room, the final dish, the surprising number, the before-and-after, all shown at second one. The payoff itself becomes the bait, and the viewer stays to learn the steps that produced it. This works especially well for any how-to or transformation content, where the result is the reason anyone would watch in the first place.

## The pattern interrupt

A pattern interrupt is a visual or audio jolt that breaks the rhythm of the feed, an unexpected motion, an odd object, a sudden sound, a surprising statement, anything that does not look like the videos around it. Because viewers scroll on autopilot, something that breaks the expected pattern snaps attention back. The interrupt buys you a second of focus; the rest of the opening has to convert that attention into a reason to stay. Used honestly it earns a look; used as random shock with no follow-through it just annoys.
